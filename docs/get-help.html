Where to get help

Services for abuse, fraud, coercion and more, with UK and international lines.

Many of these patterns can be part of abuse, fraud or coercion, so use this section even where an entry has no caution. If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number (999 in the UK). Each topic has a line that applies anywhere and a line marked UK. Outside the UK, findahelpline.com lists services by country. If someone may check your phone, browser or accounts, use a device they can’t access. Please confirm numbers before relying on them.

Danger or threats

Anywhere: In immediate danger, call your local emergency number. If you might not be able to speak, find out in advance how to reach emergency services silently where you live; this varies by country. Note the time and the words used, somewhere the other person can’t reach.

UK: 999. On a mobile, if you can’t speak, stay on the line and press 55 when prompted, and don’t hang up. Pressing 55 does not share your location. On a landline, stay on the line and listen to the operator; if you can’t speak, tap or cough, and they can connect you to the police. 101 if it is not urgent.

Abuse or coercive control

Anywhere: Contact a domestic abuse or coercive control helpline in your country (findahelpline.com lists services by country). Your local emergency number is for immediate danger. Use a device the other person can’t access.

UK: National Domestic Abuse Helpline (England, free, 24 hours): 0808 2000 247. Men’s Advice Line: 0808 801 0327. Galop (LGBT+): 0800 999 5428. Scotland: 0800 027 1234. Wales (Live Fear Free): 0808 80 10 800. Northern Ireland: 0808 802 1414. If you recognise these patterns in your own behaviour towards a partner or family member and want to change, contact Respect Phoneline (free, Monday to Friday, 10am to 5pm) on 0808 802 4040.

Money control

Anywhere: Controlling someone’s access to money can be a form of abuse. A specialist abuse service can help with safe access to money.

UK: Surviving Economic Abuse (survivingeconomicabuse.org) has information for survivors, and explains the Economic Abuse Evidence Form, which a trained money or debt adviser can send to creditors on your behalf. For urgent help use the abuse helplines above.

Intimate images

Anywhere: Don’t pay and don’t delete anything. Report it to the police and to the platform. StopNCII.org is a free tool that helps stop an adult’s intimate images being shared on taking part platforms.

UK: Revenge Porn Helpline (adults 18+, Monday to Friday, 10am to 4pm): 0345 6000 459. Under 18: Childline 0800 1111 (under 19s) or its Report Remove tool (childline.org.uk/remove); CEOP for online sexual abuse (ceop.police.uk); Internet Watch Foundation (iwf.org.uk).

Harassment or defamation

Anywhere: Keep evidence (screenshots, dates, witnesses). Persistent harassment can be reported to the police. Defamation claims have strict time limits, so get legal advice early.

UK: Police: 101 (999 if you are in danger). National Stalking Helpline: 0808 802 0300 (limited hours, so check before calling). Citizens Advice or a solicitor.

Scams and fraud

Anywhere: Stop paying and keep every message. Contact your bank or card provider straight away and report it to your national fraud-reporting service. Use a number from your card or the official website, not one the caller gave you.

UK: Call 159 (if your bank supports it) or the number on your card. Report Fraud: reportfraud.police.uk, 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland; in Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101). Forward scam texts to 7726 (free). Check that investment firms are authorised on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) Register.

Medical or authority claims

Anywhere: Don’t stop prescribed treatment because of what someone says; check with your doctor or pharmacist first. If someone claiming to be from your bank, the police or the tax office demands payment, hang up and contact the organisation yourself using an official number.

UK: Your GP or pharmacist. For scam calls, call 159 or report to Report Fraud (reportfraud.police.uk).

Consumer rights

Anywhere: Keep screenshots of the original offer and ask your card provider what payment protection applies. Check the consumer rights where you live.

UK: Citizens Advice. Credit card: Section 75 covers items costing £100 to £30,000 if you paid, even in part, by credit card. Debit card: ask your bank about chargeback (a card-scheme process, not a legal right). Online and phone purchases usually have a 14-day cooling-off period, with exceptions such as personalised goods.

Work

Anywhere: Use your employer’s formal process (HR, a written complaint) and contact a union or workplace adviser in your country.

UK: Acas helpline: 0300 123 1100 (weekdays). Your union representative. At a formal disciplinary or appeal hearing that could lead to a sanction, you can ask to be accompanied by a colleague or a trade union official.

Formal interviews

Anywhere: Before a police, legal or disciplinary interview, ask for legal or union advice.

UK: At a police station you have the right to free legal advice and can ask for the duty solicitor. If you are asked to attend a voluntary interview, ask whether free advice applies. At a formal work disciplinary hearing you can ask to be accompanied by a colleague or union official (Acas helpline: 0300 123 1100).

High-control groups

Anywhere: Keep at least one contact outside the group. Get independent advice before handing over money, housing or documents. If you feel overwhelmed, contact a crisis line in your country.

UK: The Family Survival Trust (thefamilysurvivaltrust.org, registered charity 1121388) provides information and support on coercive control and cultic groups. Citizens Advice for money or housing worries. Samaritans: 116 123 (free, 24 hours).

Gambling

Anywhere: Contact a gambling support service in your country.

UK: National Gambling Helpline (GamCare): 0808 8020 133, free, 24 hours. Live chat at gamcare.org.uk (England, Scotland and Wales).

Wellbeing

Anywhere: Talk to a doctor, counsellor or crisis line in your country.

UK: Your GP. NHS Talking Therapies (you can usually self-refer in England). Samaritans: 116 123 (free, 24 hours). NHS 111, option 2, for urgent mental health help. 999 in an emergency.

Staying private online

If someone else can see your phone, computer or accounts, a few habits can help. None of them is a guarantee.

  • Use a device they can't access, such as a library computer or a friend's phone.
  • Use a private or incognito window. It stops the browser saving that visit to its history on that device.
  • The "Leave site" button on every page jumps to a neutral page and replaces this page in the tab's history, so the Back button does not return here. Pressing Esc twice does the same. It does not delete anything already saved in your browser history, and it cannot clear history for you.
  • Downloads stay on the device until you delete them, and a downloaded PDF can show up in recent files.
  • Check what is synced. A browser signed in to an account can share history with other devices.
